Assumptions and What Would Change the Plan

What the plan rests on, how I'd test it, and how I'd adapt

Guiding principle: the client didn't want dramatic change. The existing pipeline of mandates gets delivered first. Anything new is added only where it helps that delivery, and strategic change waits until the pipeline is complete. It's tested with the client and adjusted in the first week.

I've put this plan together from outside the business, so it rests on a handful of assumptions. Here's what they are, how I'd test each one in the first week, and what I'd do if one turns out to be wrong.

Why delivery comes first

The existing mandates are work the client has already won. The revenue, the client relationships and the reputation are already on the line. Changing how people work while they're delivering puts that at risk, so the order is deliver, then light controls, then strategic change. It's also how I've worked before: at SILK I stabilised the Victorian operations first, and the acquisition and integration work came after that.

Where things aren't clear

How I'd approach it

  • Work out what I need to understand first, and who already knows it
  • Make the smallest decision that keeps live jobs moving
  • Agree how we'll know whether it worked, and check back
  • Test it against one question: does this help us deliver what we've already committed to?

An example

The general manager wins a large new job that would stretch the current crews. I'd welcome it, then do a quick capacity check with the general manager and owner before we commit to dates: crews, plant, subcontractors and the impact on jobs already promised. We'd agree a start date that protects existing clients, or what we'd need to bring in to take it on. Everything can be delivered; it's a question of cost and timing.

Disagreement

If the owner or general manager sees it differently at the end-of-week-1 review, I'd want to understand what they're seeing that I'm not, and test it against what I've found on the live jobs. It's the owner's business. My job is to give him the clearest picture I can and a recommendation, then back the decision once it's made.

What would change the plan

  • A committed job in trouble. It goes to the top of the list straight away
  • A safety gap on a live site. Fixed before anything else
  • A cash issue. Invoicing and collections take priority over everything except safety
  • The owner and general manager deciding a Level 3 change is more urgent. We'd agree how to do it without disrupting delivery, and time it outside peak
