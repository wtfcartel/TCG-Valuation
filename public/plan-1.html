1 · Getting to Know the Business

Days 1–30: people, process, numbers, systems

Plan for discussion. Written from outside the business, so every priority is a hypothesis I would test in the first 30 days, not a conclusion. The linked prototypes run on invented data.

Section 2 of 7 · use ← → keys to move between sections

I would spend most of the first month listening, on site as much as in the office. The aim is to understand how a job really moves through the business, where it gets stuck, and what people already know needs fixing.

People

  • Mick: the history, what he wants to keep, what keeps him up at night, what he wants to hand over
  • Dean: the pipeline, target markets, what customers say, where delivery has helped or hurt sales
  • One-to-ones with every manager, supervisor and the office team
  • Days on site with crews: installs, maintenance, plant yard. Same three questions everywhere: what works, what gets in your way, what would you change first
  • Calls with 5–10 key clients and a few key suppliers and subcontractors

Process: follow the work

  • Trace three or four recent jobs end to end: enquiry → quote → win → schedule → plant and crew → install → variations → handover → invoice → cash
  • Note every hand-off, every re-keying of data, every spreadsheet, and every approval that waits on one person
  • Walk the safety system: SWMS, tickets, inductions, incident reporting, toolbox talks

Numbers

  • Quoted vs actual margin on recent completed jobs
  • Work in hand and pipeline vs crew and plant capacity
  • Debtor days, unbilled work, cash forecast
  • Plant utilisation, owned vs hired, maintenance downtime
  • Safety: incidents, near misses, ticket expiries
  • Where a number doesn't exist, that is itself a finding

Systems

  • Map what is used today: accounting (e.g. Xero), job management, scheduling, timesheets, spreadsheets
  • What is entered twice, and what decisions are made without data
  • No new system is bought in the first 90 days. Fix process first, then choose tools

Output at day 30

A short written diagnostic for Mick and Dean: what I heard, what the numbers say, and the three to five priorities I propose, with the evidence behind each. We agree it together before I change anything material.
