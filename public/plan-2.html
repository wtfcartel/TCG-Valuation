2 · Priorities for the First 90 Days

Five priorities, in order, and why each matters

Plan for discussion. Written from outside the business, so every priority is a hypothesis I would test in the first 30 days, not a conclusion. The linked prototypes run on invented data.

Section 3 of 7 · use ← → keys to move between sections

Five priorities, in order. The first four are where a growing contractor typically loses money or takes risk; the fifth is what makes the other four stick. I would confirm or change this list at day 30, based on what I find.

1

Know which jobs make money, every week

Why it matters: Growth hides margin problems until the job is finished and it is too late to act. A weekly check of forecast margin on the larger live jobs lets us fix overruns, chase variations and price the next quote better.

What good looks like by day 90: Weekly margin view on every job above an agreed value; variations logged and claimed; a simple post-job review.

See the margin early-warning prototype →

2

Protect cash: quote to invoice to payment

Why it matters: Growing businesses usually run short of cash before they run short of work. Invoicing promptly at each milestone and following up debtors weekly is low-cost, fast and visible.

What good looks like by day 90: Invoice within an agreed number of days of each milestone; weekly debtor list with owners; 13-week cash forecast.

See the invoice and remittance reader →

3

Match capacity to the pipeline: crews and plant

Why it matters: If Dean wins more work than we can resource, quality and reputation suffer. If we over-hire, margin suffers. Crews, plant and subcontractors need to be planned a few weeks ahead against the pipeline.

What good looks like by day 90: Rolling four-to-six-week resource plan; owned vs hired plant decisions based on utilisation data.

See the plant and operator allocation prototype →

4

Safety and compliance: confirm the baseline

Why it matters: Non-negotiable. I need to know that tickets, inductions, SWMS and plant servicing are current before I change anything else on site. It is also the clearest way to show crews that I care about them, not just the numbers.

What good looks like by day 90: Expiry register for tickets and inductions; no-one goes on site unqualified; incidents reviewed for causes, not blame.

5

An operating rhythm with clear owners

Why it matters: Every other priority depends on it. A short weekly operations meeting, a monthly KPI pack and named owners with dates turn good intentions into follow-through, and let Mick step back from day-to-day firefighting.

What good looks like by day 90: Weekly ops meeting with an action list; monthly business review with Mick and Dean; decision rights written down.

What I would deliberately not do in 90 days

  • Restructure the team, or change roles before I understand them
  • Buy a new ERP or job-management system
  • Introduce reporting nobody uses. Every report must change a decision
