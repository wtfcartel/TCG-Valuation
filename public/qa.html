Communicating with clarity and calmness

What if Mick disagrees with your priorities at day 30?

Good: that's why the day-30 review exists. I'd ask what he's seeing that I'm not, test it against the evidence, and adjust. It is his business; my job is to give him the clearest picture and a recommendation, then commit to the decision.

What happens if you find the numbers you want don't exist?

That is a finding in itself. I'd start with what can be pulled from accounting and job records, use a simple spreadsheet before any new system, and fix data capture at the source, usually timesheets and job costing.

Leadership and people

How would you handle a long-serving supervisor who resists change?

Spend time with them first: they usually have the best knowledge of what really happens on site. Involve them in designing the fix, start with something that makes their job easier, and give them credit. If resistance continues after that, have a direct, respectful conversation about expectations.

You're coming from outside. How will you earn credibility with the crews?

By turning up on site, asking good questions, fixing practical problems quickly, and never pretending to know the work better than they do. Credibility comes from follow-through.

Judgement, assumptions and ambiguity

What assumptions have you made in this plan?

That the business is growing and some processes haven't kept pace; that margin and cash visibility can be improved; that data exists but is scattered. Each is tested in the first 30 days, and the plan changes if any is wrong.

If you could only do one thing in 90 days, what would it be?

Weekly margin visibility on live jobs, because it protects profit now and improves every future quote. Safety comes first if the baseline check turns up gaps.

Where does technology fit in a hands-on business?

Behind the process, never in front of it. The prototypes show what's possible, but nothing gets bought in the first 90 days. Fix the process first, then use simple tools that save crews and office staff time.

Commercial awareness and growth

How do you balance Dean's growth targets against delivery capacity?

With a monthly sales-and-capacity review: pipeline vs crews, plant and subcontractors over the next few months. That lets us choose which work to pursue on margin and fit, and gives early warning of when to hire or hire in plant.

How will we know you've succeeded at 90 days?

Margin visible weekly on the larger jobs, invoicing on time and debtor days trending down, a meeting rhythm that runs without me pushing it, no safety compliance gaps, and team leaders who bring problems to me early.

Tip: for any question you didn't expect, use this structure: what I would want to understand first, what I would do, and how I would know it worked.
