Q&A Preparation

The brief's four Q&A areas, with prepared answers

Guiding principle: no dramatic change. The pipeline of work Green Turf has already committed to is delivered first. Anything new is added only if it helps that delivery, and strategic change waits until the pipeline is complete. Written from outside the business, so it is tested and adjusted in the first 30 days.

Likely questions, grouped under the four areas the brief says the Q&A will explore. Each answer comes back to the same principle: deliver the committed pipeline first, then add complexity only when it helps.

Your reasoning

Why put delivering the existing pipeline ahead of improvement?

Because it is work already won: revenue, client relationships and reputation that are already promised. The business has said it wants that pipeline completed before strategic change, and I agree. Improvements made mid-delivery risk distracting the people doing the work. Finishing well also gives me the credibility and the evidence to recommend the right changes afterwards.

If you could only do one thing in 90 days, what would it be?

Make sure every committed job finishes well: on time, safely, and at the margin it was quoted at. If the safety check on live sites turns up a gap, that comes first.

Why the order you have chosen?

Deliver first, protect margin and cash second, light rhythm third, and prepare the next stage last. Each step only goes ahead if it helps the one before it.

Your assumptions

What assumptions have you made?

That there is a committed pipeline to deliver; that the business wants steadiness rather than dramatic change; and that some information is scattered across people and spreadsheets. I test each in the first 30 days and adjust the plan at the day-30 review with Mick and Dean.

What if the numbers you want don't exist?

In the first 90 days I work with what exists and ask the people running the jobs. I note the gaps for the day-90 recommendations rather than building new reporting while the team is delivering.

How you handle challenge or ambiguity

We don't want dramatic change. How does your plan reflect that?

It is the organising principle. Level 1 is delivering what is already won. Level 2 is a few light disciplines, using existing tools, only where they help that delivery. Level 3, systems, structure and growth plans, is prepared as options and not implemented in 90 days. You decide what happens next, and when.

What if Mick disagrees with your priorities at day 30?

That is why the day-30 review exists. I would ask what he is seeing that I am not, test it against what I have found on the live jobs, and adjust. It is his business; my job is to give him the clearest picture and a recommendation, then commit to the decision.

How would you handle a long-serving supervisor who is wary of you?

By helping them deliver their jobs first: clearing what is in their way, without changing how they work. Their knowledge is exactly what I need. Trust comes from follow-through, not from new processes.

Dean wins a big new job that would stretch current crews. What do you do?

Welcome it, then do a quick capacity check with Dean and Mick before committing dates: crews, plant, subcontractors, and the effect on jobs already promised. We agree a start date that protects existing clients, or what we would need to bring in.

How you adapt to new information

What would make you change this plan?

A committed job in trouble, a safety gap, or a cash issue. Any of those moves to the top straight away. Or Mick and Dean telling me a Level 3 item is more urgent than I think, in which case we agree how to do it without disrupting delivery.

How will we know you've succeeded at 90 days?

The committed pipeline delivered, or on a clear track, safely and at quoted margin; no disruption to crews or the office; milestones invoiced promptly; and an agreed list of next-stage options, with the timing in your hands.

Where does technology fit?

Later, and behind the process. The prototypes show what could be considered once the pipeline is delivered. Nothing new is introduced in the first 90 days.

For any question you didn't expect: say what you would want to understand first, what you would do, and how you would know it worked. Then check it against the principle: does this help us deliver what we have already committed to?
